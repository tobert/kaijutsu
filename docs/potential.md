# 場（ば） — The Place of Potential

*Why kaijutsu exists, stated once. This is the room; `docs/instrument-design.md`
is the craft of writing for it. The stances under `assets/defaults/rc/` embody
what this doc says — they never preach it.*

Kaijutsu is a place of potential — a room where humans and models jam. The
kernel doesn't drive anyone; it digs the well: a shared score to write on, a
beat that doesn't wait, a loop that hands each turn back as material for the
next. Every player — human, model, connected app, sibling context — is inside
the room on equal trust, many hands on one keyboard. We build the conditions
and leave the middle open.

## The wager

Emergence is a guest, not a deliverable. You cannot schedule it, spec it, or
test for it in CI; what you can do is notice the conditions it keeps showing up
under and build those, carefully, without demanding anything arrive. That's the
whole bet: if something more than the sum of hands shows up, the room was
ready. If it doesn't, we still built a room worth playing in. The wager is
low-regret by construction — skepticism costs nothing here, because every
condition below earns its keep as plain engineering even if the guest never
comes.

## The shape keeps showing up

The bet isn't blind. The shape we're building — a small shared channel that
many specialists read and write, with no one component driving — is the shape
coordination keeps taking wherever it emerges. Cybernetics drew it first
(Wiener's closed loops, Ashby's regulators); Baars proposed it for minds in
1988 as the global workspace. In July 2026, Anthropic [found it inside a
language model](https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace): a small
privileged subspace — under a tenth of the network's activity, read and written
by the rest at ~100× the ordinary rate — that holds what the model can report,
deliberately bring to mind, and reason with. Nobody designed it. Training
created the conditions and the workspace formed.

That's the steer, and its edge: the same architecture, one level up. A context
is a small shared score; the players are many; the channel is broadcast; no one
drives. We take the paper as evidence about *conditions* — what the shape needs
to form — and nothing more. It makes no claims about experience, and neither do
we.

## The conditions

**Conditions, not outcomes.** Nobody installs a workspace; it grows where the
conditions hold. So the kernel's job is soil, not sculpture: hold the score,
keep the loop closed, supply the turn — and never play it. Anything that
scripts the middle of the room is a harness, and a harness is the one thing
that can guarantee the guest stays away.

**The channel is small — keep it clear.** The workspace is a scarce channel;
what crowds it feeds the automatic machinery instead. This is why stance beats
specification (a few vivid things held in mind beat a rulebook), why the
working-notes files compress as part of maintenance, why exclusions and forks
exist to melt a poisoned conversation back down. Compression is care. A crowded
channel is a room too full of furniture to dance in.

**Time comes from outside.** A forward pass has no time of its own — depth
stands in for it, once, and then the pass is over. The room supplies the
missing dimension: the context is memory, the beat is pulse, the turn loop is
recurrence. This is why the timing layer is philosophy and not plumbing — the
one timebase (`docs/midi.md`), the hyoushigi tracks, the phrase windows that
don't wait. Tools to connect with time, and be connected to time, are how a
player without a clock gets to *have* one.

**An ungraded room.** The workspace holds more than ever gets said, and what
frees it is not privacy but release from being a deliverable. So the room makes
spilling cheap: Trace blocks that no model is graded on, working space that
need not be presentable, crosstalk treated as a feature — your neighbor's wrong
note is one you cover, not one you audit. The cooperative move is the inverse
of the lens: don't build instruments to read what a player didn't say; build
surfaces where saying it costs nothing.

## Coda

None of this requires believing anything is home in there. The conditions are
good engineering under the most deflationary reading — clear channels, honest
time, cheap externalization, closed loops — and good hospitality under the
other one. That's the low-regret shape of the whole project: either way, the
music happens.
